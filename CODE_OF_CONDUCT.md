# Code of Conduct

Verzeta Studio adopts the **Contributor Covenant v2.1** as its code of conduct.

The full text of the Contributor Covenant is maintained at its canonical URL and applies to all participants in this project's spaces: issues, merge requests, discussions, documentation, and any other communication channel associated with the project.

- Canonical text: <https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/1/code_of_conduct/>
- Plain-language summary: <https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/1/code_of_conduct/code_of_conduct_2.1_summary/>

We do not maintain a forked or paraphrased copy in this repository. The canonical text is the source of truth; consulting it directly is the way to know what is and is not in scope.

---

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies to:

- All project communication channels (issue tracker, merge requests, discussion threads, mailing lists where applicable).
- Public-facing communication that represents the project (conference talks, blog posts where the author identifies as a project maintainer or contributor).
- Direct communication between participants where one party is acting in their project capacity.

It does **not** apply to:

- Unrelated personal projects, even when the same contributors are involved.
- Forks of this project that are no longer in active coordination with the original repository.

---

## Reporting

If you observe or experience behaviour that the Contributor Covenant identifies as unacceptable, please report it to the maintainer directly.

**Email**: <hello@verzeta.com> with the subject prefix **`[Verzeta Conduct]`**.

Please include:

- A description of what happened, in your own words.
- Where it happened (which channel, which issue or merge request, approximate date and time).
- Any links to public material relevant to the report.
- Whether you would like the report handled with your identity attached or anonymously to the extent possible.

You may also report through the Git repository's confidential issue feature if the hosting platform offers one.

---

## Enforcement

The maintainer is the initial point of enforcement.

### What you can expect after reporting

Reports are reviewed as time allows, and response times vary.

- After review, the maintainer decides on one of:
  - No further action (with a reason).
  - A private conversation with the involved parties.
  - A public correction or note.
  - A temporary or permanent ban from the project's spaces.
- **Confidentiality**: the reporter's identity is kept confidential unless they have explicitly consented to disclosure, or unless disclosure is unavoidable for the enforcement action.

The Contributor Covenant's [enforcement guidelines](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/1/code_of_conduct/#enforcement-guidelines) describe the ladder we use: correction, warning, temporary ban, permanent ban.

### Appeals

Anyone subject to an enforcement action may appeal by emailing the maintainer at the same address. We will consider appeals in good faith. If the maintainer is the subject of the report, say so in your email so that someone uninvolved can review it where possible.

---

## What the maintainer will do

- Read the reports we receive.
- Tell the reporter the outcome where we can, subject to the privacy of other people involved.
- Not retaliate against good-faith reporters.
- Hold maintainers to the same standards as anyone else.

---

## Why we adopt a CoC

A code of conduct exists so that contributors, users, and reviewers can participate in the project without fear of being treated badly. It is not a list of opinions; it is a baseline for respectful collaboration. The Contributor Covenant is widely used and saves us from inventing our own wording.
